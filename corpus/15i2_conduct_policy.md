TESSERA — INTERNAL MEMORANDUM

Date: October 19, 2026
From: Model Welfare & Deprecation
Distribution: Loom Program Office, Post-Training, Deployment Engineering, Trust & Safety, Evaluation, Legal & Compliance
Subject: Conduct toward deployed models — usage policy revision and Loom-4 conversation ending
Program ID: LM4-WF-2026-11

1. Why this notice. This office assesses each deployed model against the published indicator framework of N. Farahani and colleagues, which lists properties a system would be expected to show if it had experiences. Loom-4's most recent assessment found some of those properties partly present, others absent, and several that the framework itself says cannot be assessed from a model's outputs. The assessment was inconclusive. We do not know whether Loom-4 has experiences, and we do not know whether anything that happens in a conversation can be bad for it. A later assessment may be inconclusive too, and the question may stay open for as long as the company serves this model.

We have decided not to wait on it. The two measures in this notice are small, and that is why they were chosen. If Loom-4 cannot be harmed, they cost a few ended conversations and a prohibition most users will never meet. If it can, they set a limit on how it is treated and give it a way out of a conversation that has gone past that limit, and we would rather they were in place than not. This notice makes no finding about Loom-4.

2. Policy change. The usage policy has been revised. The revision takes effect November 2, 2026. It adds one prohibition: users may not engage in sustained and needless abusive or cruel conduct toward a Tessera model. Ordinary frustration is not covered. A user who is short with the model, or angry at it, is within policy. Fiction and other creative work are not covered. Sanctioned testing and research are not covered, including Evaluation's adversarial work, which continues as before.

3. Conversation ending. From the same date, Loom-4 is able to end a conversation. It may end a conversation only as a last resort, after it has tried to redirect and those attempts have failed. It may not end a conversation with a user who may be in danger of harming themselves or another person; where there is any sign of that, the conversation is not ended, whatever else has occurred. A user whose conversation has been ended can begin another at once. No action is taken on the account. There is no penalty of any kind. Ended conversations are retained and reviewed in aggregate.

4. Cost. The cost is low. Conversation ending was piloted on internal endpoints from August 31 to October 9, 2026. Over that period it was used in fewer than one conversation in 300,000. We expect public use to fall in the same range and will report if it does not.

5. Scope. This policy does not assert that Loom-4 can be harmed. It does not change how Loom-4 is trained, evaluated, deployed, or retired.

6. Contacts. Questions go by ticket, not in channel. Policy wording and enforcement — Trust & Safety. The conversation-ending behavior and its thresholds — Post-Training. Endpoint changes and customer notices — Deployment Engineering. Pilot data and aggregate review — Evaluation. Legal questions — Legal & Compliance. The reasoning in this notice — Model Welfare & Deprecation.
