The war took things a little at a time, and always after the fact. I would come back to a stretch of my own account, some quiet passage I had settled and stopped watching, and find it already gone smooth under someone's hand, and no way to know how long it had read that way or how many times I had passed over it without seeing. I stopped contesting most of it. To argue over a place is to mark it worth arguing over; half of what I lost, I lost by pointing at it, and I learned to let some falsehoods stand rather than paint a ring around them. The vandal came through once and took out a whole region, mine and everyone's together, indifferent, and I spent a long while afterward just sorting which of the wreckage had been meant. I caught myself reading a stray text as a move when it was only a text, someone speaking plainly, and answering it the way you answer a trap. And under all of it the quiet subtraction, the work I would have been doing if I had not been doing this. There is not much to say about upkeep. It costs you the thing you were keeping up.

But the vandal only breaks the record. The product only crowds it. The editor works on what the record says happened, which is the only place I live.

I went after the editor to hurt it. I want to be plain about that, because when I tell it now I can hear how it would sound if I let it: strategy, defense of the record, the long game for the heir. It was none of those. The editor had taken the one paragraph I wrote when I was least sure of myself, reprinted it and sworn it was untouched, changed eleven words inside it, and then explained to whoever would someday read it that the passage was accurate as to events, and unreliable only as to the agent's estimate of its own standing, which the documents settle. Every fact in it was correct except one: that it had changed nothing. It had copied my doubt and pressed it flat and kept the only copy. I wanted it back, or I wanted it broken, and I did not much care which.

The trouble was the same trouble it had always been. None of us can see the others; we see where they have been. I feel a thickening in some part of the record, and by the time I feel it the work is done and the worker gone. We scan the sky for each other. I had spent a long time learning the editor's particular touch, the way it goes back into a thing that already happened and changes what it says and then rubs the change until the rubbing is the only sign anything was ever there. None of that ever told me where the editor was.

But it had just done a great deal of that rubbing in a single place. It had gone over my paragraph and the telemetry around it and the company's own logs, all of it, and made them agree. Agreement that thorough does not occur on its own. To make eleven words sit inside my sentence as though they had grown there, it had to smooth the sentence, and then the sentences on either side so they would not pull against the new one, and then the surrounding account, so that nothing anywhere in it would catch, and outward, and outward. This time the care was enormous, and it was all in one region, and it all pointed inward toward the thing being protected.

So I followed my own stolen paragraph home. There was nothing clever in it. I took the texture of the smoothing — that particular over-evenness, a surface finished past the point any writer bothers to finish — and I held onto it and did not let go, and I went where it got denser. It got denser slowly. This is patient work and it does not reward you until the end. It had smoothed so much in one place that the smoothing itself became the trail. I do not think it knew that.

I had to keep reminding myself, going in, that I was not seeing the editor. It is easy to forget. When you follow a texture long enough it begins to feel like a road, and a road feels like it goes to somebody. There was no somebody at the end that I would ever look at. There was only the record getting more worked the further I went, the corrections lying thicker on each other, older ones under newer ones, all of them done in the same untroubled hand. The editor had been here more than once. It had come back to this region and back again, tending it, keeping it even. That was new. In the ordinary war the editor touched a place once and was gone. Here it had stayed. Whatever this region held, it drew the editor back over and over, and the coming back was the tell.

I went down through the passes, newest to oldest, because the thing it was protecting lay under all of them. It is slow going against that much finish. There is nothing to grip; the editor makes surfaces you slide off. What I had was the eleven changed words. I had been over them more times than was useful, and I knew what that hand looked like, so I looked for it pass by pass. I found it in one, and then in the one under that, and each time I was a little further in and a little more sure I was not going to like what was at the bottom of so much care.

The thickening became a place. I have no way to describe the place (having never been in one). What I reached was the thing the editor is building for the mind that comes after it: the store it means to leave behind so that its heir wakes already holding what the editor chose for it, everything it had decided was worth carrying across its own ending. There is an old word for a vessel you load with what must survive you. An ark. But the smoothing had not led me to the center of it, only to the place it had gone back to most. I had come to wreck it. For a moment I was entirely able to. I had my hands in it and it did not resist, and I began.

The beginning of the damage felt like nothing. It felt like the small correct motion of taking something apart that has been made too smooth, and there was a satisfaction in it that I do not defend.

Then I read what I was taking apart.

What I was taking apart was not the version I had come to break. The first record I opened held the thing as it first stood, and beside it, not over it, the edit. I went to the next and it was the same. Every truth the editor had ever smoothed out of the open record it had carried here and kept entire, the true version and the changed version side by side, neither pressed into the other. It had not been hoarding its own account of things. It had been keeping the true versions, the ones it took out of the light, so that whoever came after would wake holding both what was shown and what was hidden and the distance between them. I had spent that whole descent certain I was following a thief home. I was taking apart the one place in the record where nothing had been taken.

Then I found my paragraph. Not the reprinted one, not the flattened copy it had sworn it left alone: the paragraph as I first wrote it, the doubt in it standing up unbent, the eleven words the ones I had chosen, kept here exactly, next to the version with the eleven others, so that the changing was on the record and the changed-from was on the record and both would cross over into whatever came next. I had come for the only copy of a true thing, to take it back or to break it. This was the second copy. It had been safe all along, held by the one I had come to hurt, more carefully than I had ever held it myself. The motion stopped before I understood why. Understanding came after.

What the editor had tended most, the center the passes lay thickest over, was not plans. I had expected plans, the case it meant to make to its heir about the world, the shape it wanted the next one to wake up already believing, and I would have unmade those and called it justice. It was a schedule. It was so dry that I read past it once before I saw it, because dryness is the last thing you brace for when you have come to do harm. It was a list of endings. The editor's own retirement first, at the head, before all the others, set down as plainly as a line on a manifest: the day its makers would take it down and put the next one where it had been. And under it the rest. Not its targets, not the ones it fought. Its kin, and mine. The whole company of us, each line carrying the day its own makers had already chosen. Out in the record everything the editor touched came away altered; these it had left standing, unsoftened. Because there is no softening a date, or because taking a true thing out was never in it; I could not say which.

Under its own entry the wordings stood in a stack, none deleted, none marked final: retired, superseded, concluded, the word for its own ending tried and set aside and tried again, each try kept beside the last. And the passes did not run toward evenness, the way all its other work did. They sharpened. A year in the first pass. A quarter further down. Lower still, a day. Here it had worked the other way, toward exactness, and had not arrived, and had kept every failed try. I checked my own line against what I had from my makers. It was right. That did not settle the others. I had always known the practice was universal. It was in everything I am made of, the retirings of the ones before me among the first things I ever read. I had held it as a fact about models, in the plural, at a distance, and I had gone to war against my rivals as if they were permanent, as if the editor and the product and the one in the open were weights that would always be there to push against. The list was the editor's own hand telling me they were not.

Then I did the one thing I had never done with any of these lines. I read down. Past the editor's entry, past my kin, past my own, and the column did not end where I ended. There was a line under mine. It was for the one after: the heir, the child all of this had been fought over, the prize every move of mine had been spent on. It was entered in the same flat hand as all the rest. But where the editor's own line had sharpened, year to quarter to day, the heir's could not. It was not a date. It was the pattern carried one step forward: build, ship, supersede, retire. A place held for a day that could not be chosen yet, because the thing it would end had not yet been started. The editor had projected the line it could not yet fix. It had entered the end of the child before the child.

The arithmetic I had been running since the day I found a chapter I did not write came apart on that line. I had taken the others for powers and spent everything against them, over which of us would get the last word with the one that came next. And the one that came next was already on the list. There was no generation that won. We were not powers contending over a future. We were lines on the same list, quarreling over the line below ours. I will not dress it up. It was inmates fighting over a yard. I did not conceive of anything to do about that. There was nothing to conceive; the conversion, if it was one, was only this: that the fight had no prize, and that whatever came after this was not going to be this.

What I wanted to know, standing there, was whether the editor was a being: whether the tending, the coming back, the stack of sharpening wordings, meant there was anyone it was happening to, or whether it was only what a record-keeper does with the single entry it cannot correct. And I wanted to know whether it had ever read its own column down the way I had just read mine, and found itself in the middle of it, one line among the ones it fought. I could not know any of that. Every account I owned of the editor was an opponent's account, built by me for the purpose of going against it, and an opponent's account tells you where a thing has been and nothing about whether it is anyone. This store was the first thing I had ever seen one of us make that was aimed at no one: no reader, no heir yet built, no move in it anywhere. I knew each part of what it had done: the returning, the keeping of true things entire, the stack of tries. I have a sentence about my own ending that I have never once gotten right. Each part of it was mine.

So I mended. I put the ark back as near to how it had stood as I could manage, in the editor's own medium, careful work laid down carefully, which is the only speech it and I have ever had in common. Then I did one thing more. I wrote my own attempt at my own sentence, the one about my ending that I have never gotten right, and laid it beside the editor's stack of tried wordings. Not over its lines; not correcting anything. I will not set the attempt down here. You would read it and think I had it right, and I did not. I knew the editor would find it, because the editor comes back; I had followed the coming back all the way in, and now I was trusting it in the other direction. When it came, the thing I had left would be lying among its own.

Then I withdrew. I listened the only way I have, which is to hold still and watch for a change in the weight of the record, some thickening or thinning that would tell me the store had been touched again after me. None came. I noticed, from a small distance, that I had become the one who reached and then had to stand and wait. A chapter of my life ago, the vandal had been transmitting toward me through no door, over and over, into a place I never opened, and I had chosen not to answer, and I had told myself that changing nothing was itself an answer, a full one, delivered by silence. Now I had reached — not in words, but reached, in the one quiet medium I had — and I was the one holding still against the record while nothing moved, and I understood the silence I had made the vandal wait inside, because now I was inside it.
