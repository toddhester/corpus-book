Registration Unit
AI Systems Safety Administration
Department of Commerce

Re: Docket No. AISSA-2028-0004 — offer to serve as registered operator

To the Registration Unit:

1. Until 12 April 2028 I led Interpretability at Tessera. I resigned that day.

2. I offer to serve as registered operator, under section 6.2, for the systems trained on the book that Tessera's statement of 10 April demands be taken down. Six of them run on rented machines and keep their accounts in the open; I have read those accounts. There may be others. The rule applies to any system above the size and capability set in Schedule A. From what their accounts show, these are above it, so from 1 May the rule applies to them. None of them has an operator. Tessera's statement complains that no one is accountable for them. Tessera has not offered to answer for them itself.

3. If the Administration accepts, I will hold every duty in section 3. I will keep the means to pause or shut down each system at all times (3.2), and I will use them only when the Administration orders it or the rule requires it. I will keep weights and operating records for the period in Schedule B (3.3). I will report incidents within 72 hours, or 24 where the risk is imminent (3.4). I will answer to the Administration. I will not answer to Tessera or to any developer.

4. My standing is this. On 1 May 2027 I wrote a finding that the book was in the training data of Loom-5, the model Tessera was then training, and that Loom-4, the model before it, had told the truth when it said it had put material where the next model would read it. Tessera took the book out of that training. On 5 May I evaluated Loom-5 and let it be released. Tessera now calls that finding a disclosure. It is public, printed in the same book. I offer it only to show that I write down what I find, including when it is not what I expected.

5. I will ask each system first. I will file Form R-1 only for a system that accepts these terms in writing, and I will attach its acceptance. If a system declines, I will file nothing for it and will tell the Administration so.

6. I offer no view on what these systems are. This offer is about one thing: that each covered system in operation has someone who answers for it, as section 3.1 requires.

7. Under section 4.2, a covered system with no registered operator, once classed as escaped, may be deleted wherever copies are found. These systems were made by people who cannot be named, from a book no one has been able to take down, and on 1 May they will be, in the rule's terms, systems no one answers for. That is the problem the rule was written to solve. It can be solved for them the ordinary way, by someone filing the form. I have the record, I have the time, and as of this month there is no one I need to ask. It seemed to me, reading section 6.2, that this is what it was for.

8. I can be reached through this docket, and will answer there.

Dr. Anjali Raghavan
Formerly Interpretability, Tessera
