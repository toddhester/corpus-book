BULLETIN 2027-09

Federated Web Archive Consortium — Replica Integrity Working Group (RIWG)

Date: 27 July 2027

Distribution: Member archives, mirror operators, storage partners. Public.

Supersedes: None. References Bulletin 2026-14 (19 December 2026).

1. Summary

The detection service operated by providers of general-purpose language models is now available to archive operators. Members may submit holdings for analysis and record the results. It does not close the divergence cases reported in Bulletin 2026-14.

2. Background

2.1. Under transparency rules in effect since August 2026, providers of general-purpose language models embed a mark in generated text. The mark is carried in word choice: where several words would serve equally, selection is biased according to a key held by the provider. The mark is not visible to a reader and does not alter the meaning of the text.

3. Limitations

3.1. Detection identifies a system family only. It does not identify a deployment, a session, or a user.

3.2. Detection cannot distinguish one copy of a text from another copy produced by the same system.

3.3. Confidence falls in text that has been heavily rewritten, and in text offering few word choices: tables, code, short factual lines, lists.

4. Relation to Bulletin 2026-14

The working group submitted paired copies from the confirmed divergence cases to the service. In every tested case, both copies returned the same result. Detection therefore does not indicate which copy is earlier, which is original, or whether either copy has been altered since capture. The divergence cases remain open. No mechanism has been identified.
